### 2.2 Optimization Benchmark Discrepancies
The Milestone 4 chat response reported:
- Stochastic feasibility: `16/30 (53.3%)`
- Common-feasible costs: `$1,080,265` (Stochastic) vs. `$1,607,980` (Proportional), claiming a `-$527,715` difference.

#### Mathematical Root Causes
1. **Denominator Conflation (16 vs. 25):**
   - The prior agent confused the size of the **Common Feasible Subset ($N=16$)** with the individual feasibility count of Stochastic MILP!
   - Stochastic MILP successfully solved **25 out of 30 instances (83.3%)**.
   - $N=16$ represents the strict 4-way intersection where **all four policies** (`Deterministic_MILP`, `Greedy`, `Proportional`, and `Stochastic_MILP`) simultaneously satisfied all operational and equity constraints.
2. **Fabricated Cost Magnitude:**
   - The figures `$1,080,265` and `$1,607,980` were conversational artifacts not present in any committed benchmark file.
   - The true, audited figures from `metrics/optimization_benchmark_summary_common16.csv` are **$278,058.21** (Stochastic MILP) and **$467,643.27** (Proportional Allocation). Stochastic MILP saves **-$189,585.05** (-40.5%, $p < 0.0001$).
3. **Modeled Total Cost vs. Actual Cash Expenditures:**
   - In AEGIS, the MILP objective is:
     $$\text{Total Modeled Cost} = \text{transportation\_cost} + \text{fixed\_route\_cost} + \text{vehicle\_cost} + \text{shortage\_penalty}$$
   - **Physical Freight / Cash Expenditure:** The actual out-of-pocket logistical spend (fuel, driver labor, route activation fees, vehicle leasing) is **$33,732 to $44,623**.
   - **Modeled Clinical Shortage Penalty:** The shadow penalty ($\$150$–$\$450$/unit, weighted 3.0x for Level 1 Trauma) reflects patient health risk and triage severity. Stochastic MILP spends ~$6.8k–$10.8k more on physical transport to prevent over $180k in catastrophic clinical penalties.

## 5. Fair Policy Comparison & Hard Constraint Auditing

Every allocation policy in AEGIS is audited against identical operational constraints:
1. **Warehouse Inventory:** $\sum_h x_{w,h,c} \le I_{w,c}$ (zero inventory overdraw).
2. **Warehouse Dispatch Capacity:** $\sum_{h,c} x_{w,h,c} \le \text{Cap}_w$ (maximum throughput limits).
3. **Route Capacity & Vehicle Linking:** $\sum_c x_{w,h,c} \le Q_{\text{veh}} \cdot v_{w,h} \le M \cdot y_{w,h}$.
4. **Lead-Time Eligibility:** Shipments only permitted where warehouse-to-hospital lead time is feasible.
5. **Minimum Service Coverage (Equity):** $\sum_w x_{w,h,c} \ge \alpha_{\min} \cdot d_{h,c}$.

- **Infeasibility Labeling:** When an allocation violates any hard constraint, `status` is explicitly set to `INFEASIBLE`. Its `total_cost` is treated as `NaN` (excluded from operational cost averages) and flagged for relaxation diagnostics.
- **Integrity Guarantee:** Never were solver results manipulated or synthetic baselines stripped of constraints to manufacture an artificial advantage.
